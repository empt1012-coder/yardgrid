Zones 3–5

The University of Minnesota Extension Upper Midwest home garden care calendar does not list tasks for February. The year on that calendar starts again in early spring, as soon as the ground thaws, then mid-March through early May for starting summer vegetable seeds indoors.

For dates in your own ZIP, use the frost dates tool or what to plant this week.

Zones 6–7

Penn State Extension’s Dauphin County seasonal garden tips are written for USDA zone 6. For February they include:

  • Start onions and leeks indoors at the beginning of the month.
  • Near the end of the month, start broccoli, cabbage, cauliflower, and Brussels sprouts indoors under lights, following the seed packet.
  • For early tomatoes, start seeds under lights now to be set out in April and protected.
  • Outdoors, prune fruit trees, brambles, grapevines, and late summer-blooming shrubs. In mild weather, press back plants that heaved with the frost, and cover exposed crowns and roots of irises, peonies, rock plants, pansies, and perennials. Continue to pull winter annual weeds.

The full indoor and outdoor list is on the February source page.

Zones 8–10

These planting lists are from UF/IFAS for Florida, not for every garden in zones 8–10. North Florida (north of State Road 40) is the closer fit for many zone 8–9 gardens. Central Florida (between State Roads 40 and 70) is the closer fit for many zone 9–10 gardens.

North Florida. Easy transplants: arugula, beets, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cabbage, cauliflower, Chinese cabbage, collards, eggplant, endive, kale, kohlrabi, lettuce, peppers, Swiss chard, tomatillo, and tomatoes. Transplant with care: celery, mustard, potatoes, and spinach. Sow from seed: carrots, bunching onions, English peas, radish, and turnips.

Central Florida. Easy transplants: arugula, beets, boniato, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cabbage, cauliflower, Chinese cabbage, collards, eggplant, endive, gingers, kale, kohlrabi, lettuce, peppers, sugarcane, Swiss chard, tomatillo, and tomatoes. Transplant with care: celery, mustard, pineapple, potatoes, spinach, and sweet potatoes. Sow from seed: bush, lima, and pole beans; cantaloupes; carrots; corn; cucumbers; okra; bunching onions; English and southern peas; radish; squashes; turnips; and watermelon.

Zones 11–13

The list below is UF/IFAS South Florida (south of State Road 70), a common fit for zone 10b–11 gardens in Florida. It does not cover Hawaii, Puerto Rico, or other zone 12–13 locations.

Easy transplants: arugula, Chinese cabbage, eggplant, endive, kohlrabi, lettuce, peppers, sugarcane, Swiss chard, tomatillo, tomatoes, and tropical spinaches. Transplant with care: amaranth, calabaza, celery, long squash, luffa, papaya, pineapple, Seminole pumpkin, spinach, and sweet potatoes. Sow from seed: bush, lima, and pole beans; cantaloupes; carrots; chayote; corn; cucumbers; okra; bunching onions; English and southern peas; radish; squashes; and watermelon.

Sources: University of Minnesota Extension, “Upper Midwest home garden care schedule” (reviewed in 2026); Penn State Extension, Dauphin County Master Gardener, February seasonal garden tips (written for USDA zone 6); UF/IFAS Gardening Solutions, “Edibles to Plant in Florida”, February lists for North, Central, and South Florida.
