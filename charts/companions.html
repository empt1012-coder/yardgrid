Marigold is not a side-by-side fix

South Dakota State University Extension calls the tomato–marigold nematode claim anecdotal. Clemson, LSU, and UF/IFAS say French or African marigolds can lower root-knot nematodes only as a solid cover: no more than 7 inches apart, grown at least two months, then turned under. The next susceptible crop goes in that spot the following season. A few flowers beside a vegetable do not do that.

The planner shows this caution when a marigold shares an edge with tomato, pepper, eggplant, bean, cucumber, squash, winter squash, melon, watermelon, onion, green onion, or garlic. Potato is on the same nematode list in the sources, and it is not a plant on the grid.

Sources: SDSU P-00379; Clemson HGIC, “Root-Knot Nematodes in the Vegetable Garden”; LSU AgCenter, “Marigolds may help control some nematodes”; UF/IFAS ENY-056, “Marigolds (Tagetes spp.) for Nematode Management.” Links are in SOURCES.md.

Beans and peas do not feed the neighbor this season

Beans and peas fix nitrogen. SDSU says most of that nitrogen becomes available at the end of the season, not as a live boost to the plant next door.

The planner repeats these two notes when the plants share an edge. Nothing else is treated as a companion.
