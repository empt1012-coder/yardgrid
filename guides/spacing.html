Square-foot spacing

Each square in Bloom & Bed Garden is one foot by one foot. South Dakota State University Extension shows how to turn a seed-packet spacing into a count. Divide 12 inches by the spacing, then square the result. A plant that wants 3 inches fits 4 by 4, which is 16 per square. Six inches fits 4. Twelve inches fits 1.

That SDSU sheet (P-00379, March 2026) publishes these groups:

  • 16 per square: radishes, carrots, green onions, beets.
  • 9 per square: bush beans, spinach, garlic.
  • 4 per square: lettuce, Swiss chard, basil.
  • 1 per square: broccoli, cabbage, peppers, eggplant, parsley.

Those counts come from the Square Foot Gardening spacing method and from seed packets, as the sheet says. Other extensions use wider block spacing. Colorado State University’s GardenNotes #720 lists leaf lettuce at 7 to 9 inches and broccoli at 18 inches, and it says the closer spacing is only for improved soil with plenty of organic matter. The University of Florida IFAS guide for Northwest Florida also differs on some crops: basil at 1 per square, garlic at 4, a staked tomato at 1 per 2 squares, summer squash at 1 per 4 squares.

Bloom & Bed Garden follows the SDSU count when that sheet names the crop, and says on the plant card when another guide wants more room. For crops SDSU does not count, the grid uses the roomier published figure. A tomato occupies two squares because that is the IFAS staked-tomato number. Summer squash occupies four.

Each plant has its own page with its count, spacing notes, and planting time: plant spacing for 30 plants.

If your plants look crowded, trust the wider figure on the card next year. The square is a plan, not a rule that beats the plant.
