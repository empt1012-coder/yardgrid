How to plan a raised bed

A raised bed is a box of soil you can reach without stepping in it. University of Minnesota Extension lists practical reasons: you can garden on a patio, in a wet spot, or where an in-ground bed is not allowed. Soil in a raised bed warms earlier in spring. Several smaller beds also make it easier to rotate crops than one long bed.

Put the bed where you can water it, and match the light to the plants. UMN says tomatoes, peppers, and eggplants want a spot with at least 6 to 8 hours of sun. If the bed gets less than a few hours, the same page says you can still grow lettuce, baby kale, scallions, microgreens, radishes, and beet greens.

Colorado State University Extension describes close-row beds that are 2 to 4 feet wide, any length you want, with about a 2-foot walkway between them. A 4-foot width lets most people reach the middle from the side. Bloom & Bed Garden asks for width and length in whole feet and draws one square per foot.

UMN also notes that you can add hoops and a cover later if you want a longer season. The planner does not draw hoops. It only lays out the squares.

Sources: UMN Extension, “Raised bed gardens”; CSU Extension, GardenNotes #720, “Vegetable Planting Guide.” Links are in SOURCES.md.
