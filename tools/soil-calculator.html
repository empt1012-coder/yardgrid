How much soil a raised bed holds

A 4 × 8 foot bed filled 12 inches deep holds 32 cubic feet of soil, or about 1.19 cubic yards. That is 32 bags of 1 cubic foot, 22 bags of 1.5 cubic feet, 16 bags of 2 cubic feet, or 13 bags of 2.5 cubic feet, with the last two counts rounded up.

Measure the inside of the box, not the outside of the boards, and use the depth you will actually fill. Iowa State University Extension says to multiply the width, length, and height of the side walls. Keep the units the same, then convert to cubic feet if you measured in inches (divide cubic inches by 1,728). Bulk soil is sold by the cubic yard, and one cubic yard is 27 cubic feet.

Iowa State notes that bagged soil is typically sold in 1, 1.25, or 1.5 cubic foot bags; 2 and 2.5 cubic foot bags are also common. Divide the volume by the bag size and round up so you do not come up short. University of Minnesota Extension says buying in bulk by the cubic yard is generally much cheaper than buying individual bags, which matters once you get near a cubic yard or more.

For the mix itself, University of Minnesota Extension describes an ideal raised-bed mixture as around one-half to two-thirds topsoil and one-third to one-half plant-based compost, and says potting soil is a poor fit for a raised bed because it dries out too quickly. Iowa State University Extension describes an excellent mix as equal parts topsoil, organic matter (well-rotted manure, compost, or peat), and coarse sand, or about 70% topsoil and 30% compost. The 60 / 30 / 10 split in the calculator is only a common rule of thumb for a topsoil, compost, and aeration blend.

If you already know the bed size, you can lay out the plants in the planner. There is also a short guide on how to plan a raised bed.

Sources: Iowa State University Extension, “How do I determine the amount of soil needed to fill a container or raised bed?” and “What would be a good soil mix for a raised bed?”; University of Minnesota Extension, “Raised bed gardens.” More detail is in SOURCES.md.
