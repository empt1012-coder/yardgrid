What these dates mean

The date is the 50% NOAA climate normal at the nearest station with both 32°F dates within 80 km of the ZIP’s Census point. “50%” means that in the 1991–2020 period, a 32°F reading had happened by that date in about half of the years. A later spring frost, or an earlier fall frost, still happens in the other half. A USDA zone is a winter-low band, not a frost date, so two places in the same zone can have different dates.

A short guide on how to use your frost date explains how extension planting windows count from it. Once you have a date, you can see what to plant this week, open the planting calendar, or plan a bed.

Sources: NOAA NCEI U.S. Climate Normals 1991–2020, last spring and first fall 32°F dates at 50% probability; 2023 USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map ZIP listings (PRISM Group, Oregon State University). Census 2024 ZCTA points place each ZIP. More detail is in SOURCES.md.
