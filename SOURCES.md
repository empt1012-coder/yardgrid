# Sources

Checked 18 November 2026. Plant numbers in the planner come from these pages. If a figure is not here, it is not in the tool.

## Spacing, days, and planting windows

- South Dakota State University Extension. Robin Buterbaugh, Nick Volesky, Amanda Bachmann, and Kristine Lang. “Square-Foot Garden Sample Planting Layout.” P-00379. March 2026.
  https://extension.sdstate.edu/sites/default/files/2026-03/P-00379-v2.pdf
  Plants per square foot, the 12-inch formula, and the sample-bed days and companion notes (including the line that calls marigold–tomato nematode claims anecdotal, and the note that bean and pea nitrogen is mostly available at the end of the season).

- Colorado State University Extension. “Vegetable Planting Guide.” GardenNotes #720.
  https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/vegetable-planting-guide/
  Hardy, semi-hardy, tender, and very tender windows. Block spacing, typical days to harvest, and transplant age. Bed width of 2–4 feet and 2-foot paths. Soil temperature as a planting signal.

- Colorado State University Extension. “Vegetable Gardening in the Mountains.”
  https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/vegetable-gardening-in-the-mountains/
  Direct-seed kale, mustard, and other frost-tolerant crops four weeks before the last frost. Leeks sown indoors 6–8 weeks before the last frost and planted out two weeks before.

- Colorado State University Extension. “Growing Annual Plants from Seed.”
  https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/growing-annual-plants-from-seed/
  Marigold: 4 weeks indoors, and marked for direct seeding about two weeks before the average frost-free date.

- UF/IFAS Leon County Extension. “Vegetable Square Foot Planting Guide for Northwest Florida.”
  https://sfyl.ifas.ufl.edu/media/sfylifasufledu/leon/docs/Vegetable-Square-Foot-Planting-Guide-for-Northwest-Florida-PDF-mcj.pdf
  Square-foot counts and days for crops the SDSU sheet does not count, plus the conflicts named on each plant card (basil, garlic, tomato, summer squash, melons, winter squash, watermelon). Florida calendar months are not used as frost offsets.

- University of Minnesota Extension. “Planting the vegetable garden.”
  https://extension.umn.edu/planting-and-growing-guides/planting-vegetable-garden
  Warm-season transplants, including basil, wait until after the last frost. Carrots, beets, and radishes are direct seeded.

- University of Minnesota Extension. “Raised bed gardens.”
  https://extension.umn.edu/planting-and-growing-guides/raised-bed-gardens
  Why a raised bed, 6–8 hours of sun for tomatoes, peppers, and eggplants, and the short list of crops that can take less light.

- University of Minnesota Extension. “Growing tomatoes in home gardens.”
  https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-tomatoes
  Tomatoes need full sun and a frost-free season. Stake or cage at planting. Set out after frost danger has passed and the soil has warmed.

- University of Minnesota Extension. “Growing herbs in home gardens.”
  https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-herbs
  Most herbs need at least six hours of direct sun. Transplant herbs after frost danger has passed.

## Neighbor notes

- SDSU P-00379, above. Anecdotal marigold and basil notes. Bean and pea nitrogen timing.

- Clemson Cooperative Extension Home & Garden Information Center. “Root-Knot Nematodes in the Vegetable Garden.”
  https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/root-knot-nematodes-in-the-vegetable-garden/
  French and African marigolds can reduce root-knot nematodes when planted solidly, no more than 7 inches apart, grown at least two months, then turned under. The next susceptible crop goes in that spot the following season.

- LSU AgCenter. “Marigolds may help control some nematodes.”
  https://www.lsuagcenter.com/portals/communications/news/news_archive/2009/may/get_it_growing/marigolds-may-help-control-some-nematodes
  Planting marigolds beside a susceptible crop does not stop root-knot nematodes from finding that crop. A cover crop is the use that works. Susceptible examples named there include tomatoes, snap beans, cucumbers, and squash.

- UF/IFAS. “Marigolds (Tagetes spp.) for Nematode Management.” ENY-056.
  https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/NG045
  Marigolds need at least two months on the same site as the following crop.

No other companion pairs are shown. Charts that only say “likes” or “dislikes” were left out.
